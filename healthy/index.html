The salad that loses to a sandwich

The Greek Salad is a cheese-and-dressing salad, not a diet plate. At 630 calories and 8g of protein, it loses to the Mediterranean Veggie sandwich: 520 calories and 18g of protein for $8.12. The playbook

Five rules that beat any list

Lists age out every menu refresh. These five moves work on whatever Panera is serving this season, and each one is a decision you make at the counter, not a fact you have to memorize.

1

The salad rule

Dressing on the side, always

Every salad number on this page is the standard build with full dressing. Asking for the dressing on the side hands the pour back to you, and it is the single biggest lever on both calories and sodium a salad has. Order it that way before you are asked, not after.

Full dressingis priced into every number above
2

The soup rule

Cup, not bowl

Soup calories scale with the pour. The cup is the honest portion for a light meal; the bowl is the comfort order wearing the same name. Decide which meal you are having before the register asks. Even the lightest soup carries 1,570mg of sodium, so the cup is also the sodium decision.

180 calChicken Noodle, the lightest cup decision
3

The side rule

Apple, not chips

The kettle chips that ride along are 150 verified calories before the meal starts, and the cookie case adds 390 more if it joins. The apple is the side that keeps a light order light. This is the cheapest healthy decision on the menu: it costs nothing and happens by default only if you say it.

150 calkettle chips, verified in our menu numbers
4

The broth rule

Broth, not cream

The soup case splits cleanly in two. Broth-based soups are the light orders; cream-based soups are the comfort orders, and the gap is not small: Chicken Noodle 180 calories against Broccoli Cheddar 420, with the broth soup carrying more protein. Pick the family first and the flavor second.

5

The bowl rule

Skip the bread bowl

The bread bowl turns a soup into a sandwich that ate the soup. It adds several hundred calories to an order whose whole point was being light, on top of the soup’s own sodium. Exact bread-bowl nutrition is not in our checked menu numbers, so we do not print a number we cannot stand behind: check the current guide in the Panera app, and treat the bowl as the meal, not the vessel.

The sodium footnote

The real story is not calories

The real healthy-eating story at Panera Bread is sodium, not calories. The Toasted Italiano, a hot deli sandwich, carries 4,110mg: 179% of the 2,300mg daily value.

Read enough of the menu and a pattern shows up: Panera’s calories are manageable and its protein is honest. The number that quietly runs the table is sodium.

4,110mgToasted Italiano sandwich · 179% of the daily value

One sandwich, nearly two days of salt

The FDA daily value for sodium is 2,300mg. The Toasted Italiano spends 179% of it in a single sandwich, before a side or a drink. It is the extreme case, but the direction is menu-wide.

Even the healthy picks on this page run 1,700mg to 1,980mg of sodium, and the lightest real meal, the Chicken Noodle, still carries 1,570mg. The Steel Cut Oatmeal at 170mg is the rare exception that proves the rule.

The decision: treat healthy at Panera as a one-meal sodium budget. Pick the gentle item, keep the rest of the day light on salt, and use the full nutrition table when you need to filter the whole menu by the number.
