    The part no price list gives you

    The office order playbook

    Five rules, in order. Run them top to bottom and the order mostly handles itself. Skip rule 3 and you will spend the meeting sorting lids instead of eating.

    1

    Count heads, then add one or two boxes

    Headcounts lie politely. Someone brings a guest, someone who declined gets hungry at noon, and one box always gets built wrong. The extra boxes are cheaper than the apology tour.

    Do this: final count plus two. Make both extras Mediterranean Veggie boxes: they suit the widest range of eaters and keep without drama.

    2

    Always include vegetarian boxes

    Do not wait for someone to ask. In any room over eight people, at least one vegetarian is present, and the person least likely to announce it is the one who ends up with chips and a cookie for lunch.

    Do this: at least one in five boxes is Mediterranean Veggie, even when every RSVP says otherwise. It is the safe vegetarian box: lightest main, familiar build.

    3

    Label every box with a name

    Typically, boxed lunches can carry individual names, and this is the feature that justifies the format. Unlabeled boxes turn a five-minute handoff into a lid-opening ceremony while the food gets cold.

    Do this: name on the lid, dietary note under the name. Send the name list with the order, not in a follow-up email the cafe will never connect to it.

    4

    Match the box to the eater

    Sandwich boxes carry the protein and the calories. Salad boxes carry the lighter mains, plus bread and the same cookie. Sending every person the same box wastes the one advantage the format gives you.

    Do this: sandwich boxes for the hungry, salad boxes for light eaters, Half and Half for the undecided. One line in the invite: sandwich, salad, or half and half. That is the whole survey.

    5

    Order 24 to 48 hours ahead and confirm the window

    Typically, this is the safe window, and the morning-of call is part of the order, not paranoia. Cafes build boxed lunches around the regular lunch rush: a confirmed window is what keeps your 12 boxes out of that crush.

    Do this: order at catering.panerabread.com, screenshot the delivery window, then confirm by phone the morning of. No serving setup is needed after that: hand the boxes out and start.

    Before you pay the premium

    Boxed lunch or platter?

    Boxed lunches are the convenient format, not the cheap one. Here is the honest split, in the order offices actually hit it.

    If the order looks like thisBoxed winsPlatter wins
    Mixed dietary needs in one roomVegetarian, light eaters, and big appetites together
    Boxed wins

    Each box is built and labeled for one person. Nobody has to pick through a shared tray for what they cannot eat.

    Shared pans force one build on everyone. You will order extra pans to cover the same ground.

    No time to set upClient meeting, interviews, training starts on the hour
    Boxed wins

    Hand them out. No serving, no portioning, no keeping soup hot while people drift in.

    Pans and packs need table space, serving tools, and someone willing to own the setup.

    Budget is the decisionFeeding many people on a fixed number

    At $12.89 to $18.19 per person typically, boxed is the premium format. Convenience is what you are buying.

    Platter wins

    Bagel packs near $1.42 per person and group soup pans near $5.41 per person (typical), stretch much further. Run the numbers in the planner.

    Exact headcount, food must look cared forThe impression matters as much as the meal
    Boxed wins

    A named box per person reads as planned. It is the format that looks like someone thought about the room.

    Platters read casual. Right for team lunches, wrong for the client you are trying to land.
