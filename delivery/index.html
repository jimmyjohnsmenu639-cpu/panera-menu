Panera Delivery: How to Order Online (2026)

Panera ended its own in-house delivery program. Today delivery is fulfilled two ways: through Panera's own website and app, and through three independent delivery companies, DoorDash, Uber Eats, and Grubhub, each with its own fees and menu prices. Minimums are $8–$10 on Panera’s channel, and every fee and price below varies by location. Once you know the fees, the menu finder on our homepage helps you pick food that is worth having delivered.

Most search results give you one fee number and call it a day. Delivery pricing is a stack of four layers, and the route you order through changes how the layers add up. This page lays out the whole stack before you tap order.

Money

What delivery really costs

Almost every search result quotes one fee and calls it the price. The honest truth is four layers that stack on each other. From Panera’s own published delivery terms and the apps’ pricing, here is the full stack.

  1. 1

    Higher delivery menu prices

    Before anything else: Panera’s delivery menu prices are set higher than in-café prices, per its published delivery terms. The food costs more before the first fee line ever appears.

  2. 2

    A $1 delivery fee

    On Panera’s own channel, a flat $1 delivery fee applies to every delivered order. Select California cafés add a $2 supplemental charge on top of that.

  3. 3

    A convenience or service fee

    Panera’s channel adds a Convenience Fee; DoorDash, Uber Eats, and Grubhub add their own service fees. The amounts vary by café and order: this is the line nobody can quote for you in advance.

  4. 4

    The driver tip

    On top of all three layers: the tip. The honest way to price delivery is to look at the fees first, then tip, so you see what you are actually spending.

Illustrative example: not a quote A $12 sandwich can land near $18–$20 delivered once all four layers stack: menu markup, delivery fee, convenience fee, and tip. Your checkout may land higher or lower. Fees and pricing vary by café.

Check the pre-tip total before confirming

Every number on every website is an average, a range, or last month’s deal. The checkout screen is the only honest total: compare it to pickup before you pay.

One more fee-related fact worth knowing: delivery also runs on a $8–$10 minimum order on Panera’s channel (location-dependent), and delivery areas are limited: if your address is outside the café’s zone, the app won’t let you checkout.

Practical tips

Order smart for delivery

Delivery is a different sport than eating in. Some menu items arrive perfect: some arrive sad. Order like you know the difference.

Order soup: it travels best

Soups are the delivery-native menu: hot, sealed, and built for a box. Broccoli Cheddar and Homestyle Chicken Noodle are among Panera’s most-ordered delivery items on DoorDash for a reason: the bowl arrives intact.

Take the bread bowl

Bread bowls hold heat and structure better than plain bread packaging does: the edible container is also the sturdiest one. A bread bowl at the door tastes closer to in-café than anything else.

Salads: dressing on the side

Dressing rides in the bag for 30 minutes and turns greens into a wilted mess. Order it on the side, dress at the door, and the salad survives.

Frozen drinks: skip unless you’re close

Smoothies and frozen drinks melt in traffic: from far away, they arrive as flavored water. If the café is minutes away, go ahead; otherwise stick to the regular drinks.

Bakery items travel fine

Bagels, sourdough, and cookies are room-temperature peripherals: nothing melts, nothing wilts. Round out the order with them in confidence.

Feeding a group? Check catering

Six-plus people is catering territory: boxed lunches and pans usually beat building half a dozen separate delivery orders, and catering delivery is built for it.

What people actually order for delivery

Per DoorDash’s store pages, Panera’s most-ordered delivery items include Broccoli Cheddar Soup, Grilled Cheese, Mac & Cheese, Tuna Salad Sandwich, and Homestyle Chicken Noodle Soup. The pattern is clear: items that hold heat and travel in a bowl dominate.

Logistics

Hours, scheduling, and catering

The three logistical details that decide whether your order actually works tonight.

Delivery hours

Vary by café, and are often shorter than the café’s open hours. Don’t guess from the door sign: confirm your café in the app before planning around delivery.

Scheduled delivery

Yes, where offered. Third-party apps let you pick a time slot at checkout so the food arrives when you want it. The café still needs to be inside delivery hours for the slot you pick.

Catering delivery

A separate track from regular orders: boxed lunches and pans, ordered ahead, delivered with catering logistics. If you’re feeding a crowd, this is your route.
