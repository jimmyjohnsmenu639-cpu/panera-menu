The first decision

Boxed or platter?

Before you touch a price list, pick the format. It is the single biggest cost swing in catering: the same 12 people can run $65 or $190 depending on this one choice.

Individual format

Boxed lunches

$13 to $18 per person, on catering listings

  • Win when dietary needs are mixed: each box is one person’s meal, built and labeled separately.
  • No serving setup: hand them out, done. Nothing to serve, portion, or keep warm.
  • Professional settings: client meetings, interviews, anywhere a shared tray looks casual.
  • Headcount is exact: you order 12 boxes for 12 people, no waste math.

The trade: it is the priciest format per person, and per-person prices here come from marketplace reports, not our checked menu numbers.

Shared format

Pans, packs & platters

$1.42 to $5.41 per person, verified prices, servings typical

  • Budget matters most: bagel packs and soup pans stretch further per person than any box.
  • Casual grazing: team lunches, training days, and rooms where people drift in and out.
  • Everyone eats the same: one soup pan flavor is fine when the group is not picky.
  • You control the buffer: round pans up for big appetites instead of paying per head.

The trade: you own the setup and the serving math, and the serving counts are typical, so the 10% buffer is not optional.

The honest line: boxed lunches cost about $13 to $18 per person (on catering listings), while pans and packs stretch much further per person on verified prices. Pay the boxed premium for convenience and dietary control; pay for pans when the budget is the decision.

The fine print

What the $1.42 does not cover

Drinks. A half gallon of tea or lemonade is $7.66 to $10.11 and covers about eight cups (typical). Budget one per 6 to 8 guests, or breakfast math quietly doubles.

Protein. Bagels are a base, not a meal. The Asiago Everything single packs 370 cal and real heft at $1.98 if you are building by hand instead of by pack.

How to order

Four steps, no surprises

Catering goes wrong on logistics, not on menu choices. Here is the order that works, with the fee and minimum figures labeled typical, not confirmed.

1

Count your guests

Use the planner above: headcount first, format second, flavors last. Add the 10% buffer for plus-ones and big appetites.

2

Pick the format

Boxed for mixed dietary needs and zero setup; pans and packs when the budget rules. The boxed-or-platter call takes two minutes and saves real money.

3

Order 24 to 48 hours ahead

Typically, that is the safe window. Order at catering.panerabread.com and call the cafe for anything larger than two packs.

4

Delivery or pickup

Typically, standard delivery starts around $5 and catering delivery around $15 and up. Pickup skips the fee entirely: for two packs or less, it is usually the right call.
