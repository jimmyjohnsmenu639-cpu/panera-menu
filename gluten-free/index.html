The one distinction that matters

Gluten-conscious is not gluten-free

Panera chose its words carefully. Understanding the gap between these two terms is the whole decision.

The strict standard

Gluten-free

Prepared without gluten and protected from cross-contact: separate surfaces, separate utensils, controlled ingredients from prep to plate.

Panera does not claim this. No menu item carries a gluten-free guarantee, and there is no dedicated prep area in a bakery-cafe built around fresh bread.

What Panera actually says

Gluten-conscious

Made without gluten-containing ingredients, in a shared kitchen where wheat flour is in the air and on the counters.

Panera uses the term gluten-conscious for items made without gluten-containing ingredients. Per Panera’s official allergen guide, that does not mean gluten-free, and Panera cannot guarantee against cross-contact in its shared kitchens.

In Panera’s own framing: items can be made without gluten-containing ingredients, but the company cannot guarantee that any item is free from cross-contact in its bakery-cafes.

That framing fits readers who avoid gluten by preference or mild sensitivity and judge the risk themselves. It does not fit celiac disease. The next section is blunt about why.

The verdict to read first

If you have celiac disease, Panera is not considered safe

We put this near the top because it is the fact that changes the order. Shared prep areas, shared utensils, and airborne flour in a working bakery mean cross-contact is not a rare accident at Panera. It is the normal condition of the kitchen.

Shared kitchen, always

Salads, soups, and egg dishes are prepped in the same space as sandwiches and bakery production. There is no separate gluten-free station to request.

Flour is in the air

Panera bakes on site. Airborne wheat flour settles on surfaces throughout the day, which is exactly the exposure celiac readers must avoid.

Your call, with full facts

Readers with mild sensitivity often navigate Panera by personal judgment. If you have celiac disease, talk to your doctor about your own risk tolerance, and weigh this kitchen honestly.

If that verdict ends Panera for you, it did its job here. If you avoid gluten for other reasons and accept the shared-kitchen risk, the rest of this page shows how to order with the fewest moving parts.

The no list

What Panera cannot do for you

Search engines will ask these questions a thousand times. The answers are short, so we collected them in one place.

No gluten-free bread.

Every loaf, roll, and bagel contains wheat

There is no substitute bread to build a sandwich on, and no gluten-free bagel in the bakery case. A sandwich without the bread is a salad with extra steps: order the salad instead, where the format works in your favor.

The whole bakery case is out.

Pastries, muffins, cookies, and scones

All bakery items contain wheat, and they share cases and tongs with everything else in the display. There is no corner of the bakery case that a gluten-avoiding reader should treat as safe.

  • Sandwiches and flatbreads: built on wheat bread, every one.
  • Mac and cheese: wheat pasta, no cited alternative.
  • Bread bowls: the bowl itself is the wheat.

Order it safely

Five steps, in order

The safest Panera order is a process, not a menu item. Run these five steps every time, including at the cafe you always visit: recipes and staff rotate even when the building does not.

1

Start in the app

Open the allergen filter in the Panera app and build your order there first. It reflects the current official allergen guide, not a guide written last season.

2

Say the removals out loud

“No croutons” and “dressing on the side” are spoken requests, confirmed at the counter. Defaults put both back in.

3

Pick simple builds

Fewer ingredients means fewer recipes to check and fewer chances for a swap you did not check. The frittata beats the twelve-component sandwich.

4

Tell the staff

Say what you need and why, plainly. Staff can flag your order, change gloves, and confirm prep. Clear requests get careful handling.

5

When in doubt, skip it

If an ingredient cannot be confirmed, the answer is the fruit cup, the whole fruit, or a different restaurant. No lunch is worth guessing about.
