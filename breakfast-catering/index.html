Panera Breakfast Catering: The Morning Meeting Playbook (2026)

Bagel packs, coffee totes, and boxed breakfasts: the per-person math and the exact order for 6, 12, or 25 people. The morning items come from the same menu the rest of the day uses, priced there by the item.

Every catering site we checked lists prices and stops there. None of them tells you what a room of 12 actually costs per person, or how many coffee totes that room needs. This page does: three cost anchors, three meeting-size formulas, and one coffee rule, with every figure labeled verified or typical.

How to read the labels: Verified prices come from our menu numbers of 9-city averages checked September 30, 2026. Typical figures (serving counts, coffee tote and boxed breakfast prices, lead times, fees) come from catering listings, because Panera does not publish them. Serving sizes, fees, and minimums always carry the typical label; only checked menu prices are called verified.

A coffee tote is Panera's boxed coffee for groups: 120 fluid ounces, about ten servings, brewed fresh to order. A boxed breakfast pairs one breakfast sandwich or pastry with a fruit cup, packed per person. Breakfast catering is the morning side of Panera's catering service; the lunch side is covered in the catering planner.

Start with the anchors

The per-person math

Three numbers run every breakfast catering decision at Panera. Learn these and the formulas below are just arithmetic.

The cheapest way to feed a room

$1.42 per person

Bagel Pack $18.46 ÷ 13 bagels = $1.42. Two cream cheese tubs are included in the pack (typical).

No catering format at Panera, and few anywhere, feeds a person for less. If the budget is the decision, this is the anchor everything else is compared against.

Price verified · Serves 13 typical

The coffee line

~$3.40 per person

Coffee tote ~$34 ÷ 10 servings = ~$3.40. One tote is 120 fl oz, per ezCater and bringmethat reports.

Coffee costs more per head than the bagels do. That is normal, and it is why the rule of 10 below counts coffee drinkers, not people.

Typical

The zero-effort option

$7.69–$11.29 per person

Boxed breakfast: a sandwich or pastry plus a fruit cup, built and labeled per person, on catering listings.

You pay roughly five to eight times the bagel math for zero setup and exact headcount control. Worth it for client meetings; hard to justify for a Tuesday standup.

Typical

The middle path: a simple breakfast order typically runs about $6 to $10 per person, per paneramenuguide. That is the band most office orders land in once coffee and one hot item join the bagels.

Get the coffee right

Coffee: the rule of 10

Every figure here is per ezCater and bringmethat reports, because Panera does not publish tote specs. The rule itself is the part worth memorizing.

1 tote = 10 servings

That is the unit. A tote is not “coffee for the room”; it is 10 servings, and rooms drink more than one serving each.

120 fl oz

The volume behind the count: about 12 oz per serving. Useful when you are comparing against brewing your own.

1 tote per 8–10 drinkers

Order per coffee drinker, not per person: not everyone in the room drinks coffee, and drinkers rarely stop at one cup.

15 min per tote

Allow roughly 15 minutes of prep per tote (typical). Three totes for an 8 a.m. meeting means ordering well ahead, not at 7:45.

Worked example: 15 people, roughly 10 coffee drinkers: 2 totes. The second tote feels like a luxury when you order it and like foresight at 8:40 a.m.

Run the morning

The morning timeline

Breakfast catering fails on timing more often than on food. Three checkpoints, all typically, keep the morning boring in the good way.

1

24 to 48 hours ahead

Place the order

That lead time is typical (typical), and it is what the 15-minute-per-tote coffee math quietly demands. Pick your formula above and order the whole thing at once, bagels, totes, and add-ons together.

2

The day before

Confirm the window

Confirm the morning delivery window with the cafe, and confirm the delivery fee at the same time: fees vary by cafe and are not published. A 9 a.m. meeting wants food down by 8:30.

3

Morning of

Hot items last

Put bagels, fruit, and coffee out first; soufflés and breakfast sandwiches go out last so they are eaten hot, not held warm. Cold food forgives an early start; hot food does not.
